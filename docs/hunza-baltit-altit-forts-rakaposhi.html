Hunza's two forts and the mountain that watches over them

Baltit and Altit forts tell the story of the Hunza kingdom, and Rakaposhi's 7,788-metre peak towers over them both. A short guide.

By Maps and Musings · 22 October 2026

High in the Karakoram, the Hunza Valley in Gilgit-Baltistan is one of Pakistan's most visited places. Two old forts stand above its villages, and one of the world's great mountains rises across the valley.

Baltit Fort

Baltit Fort sits on a rocky outcrop above Karimabad, looking over the Hunza River. Its history goes back more than 700 years. It was home to the Mirs, Hunza's hereditary rulers, until 1945, when the last Mir moved to a new palace. After that, the fort fell into disrepair.

The Aga Khan Trust for Culture began planning its conservation in 1985, and the restored fort opened as a museum and cultural centre in 1996. The restoration won a UNESCO Asia-Pacific Heritage Award in 2004. Its architecture mixes Tibetan, Balti and Central Asian styles, a reminder that Hunza was linked to the old Silk Road trade routes.

Altit Fort

A short distance along the valley, Altit Fort is older still. Built in the 11th century, it is described as the oldest surviving monument in the region and was the first royal residence of Hunza's rulers. It stands on rock more than 1,000 feet above the Hunza River. The Aga Khan Trust for Culture restored the fort and the old village of Altit Khun around it, and the work received a UNESCO Asia-Pacific award in 2011.

Rakaposhi

Look south-west from Karimabad and the view is filled by Rakaposhi, 7,788 metres high. Its snow-covered face rises almost straight out of the valley, which makes it one of Pakistan's best-known views.

Visiting

Karimabad is reached by the Karakoram Highway from Gilgit. Both forts are open to visitors as museums; check opening times locally before you go, especially in winter.
