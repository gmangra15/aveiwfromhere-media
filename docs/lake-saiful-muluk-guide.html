Lake Saiful Muluk: the high lake of a fairy-tale legend

Near Naran in the Kaghan Valley, Lake Saiful Muluk sits about 3,224 metres high beneath glaciers and Malika Parbat, and carries one of Pakistan's best-loved legends.

By Maps and Musings · 25 October 2026

At the northern end of the Kaghan Valley, above the town of Naran in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa's Mansehra district, lies one of Pakistan's most visited and most photographed lakes: Lake Saiful Muluk.

High and cold

The lake sits at about 3,224 metres (10,578 feet) above sea level and is often described as Pakistan's highest alpine lake. Its water is clear, cold and greenish-blue, fed by the snow and glaciers of the surrounding peaks, including Malika Parbat, the "Queen of the Mountains".

The lake supports brown trout, as well as algae, water plants and phytoplankton, according to Khaleej Times.

The legend

The lake takes its name from a romance retold for centuries. Prince Saif-ul-Malook of Egypt fell in love with a fairy princess, Badi-ul-Jamal. In one version, he searched for six years and prayed for forty days before finding her at the lake.

A white giant, Safaid Deyo, held the princess captive. When the lovers escaped, his fury caused a great flood. Locals say nearby Ansoo Lake, "the lake of tears", was formed from the giant's tears, and that on the fourteenth night of each lunar month, around the full moon, the prince and princess still dance above the water.

The story was told in a celebrated Punjabi poem by the Sufi poet Mian Muhammad Bakhsh, which helped make it famous across the region.

Visiting

  • When to go: June to September. Daytime temperatures are around 15–20°C, dropping to about 3°C at night. In winter, heavy snow freezes the lake and access is difficult.
  • Getting there: The lake is reached from Naran. Ponies and horses are available for visitors who prefer not to walk the last stretch.
  • Boating: Boats are offered, but the lake's depth is not well documented and rescue equipment is limited, so take care.
  • Camping: Visitors often camp nearby; nights are cold, so bring warm clothing.

Look after it

Saiful Muluk's popularity has brought litter and crowds in peak season. Carry your rubbish back with you and stay on established paths, so the lake keeps its magic for the next visitors.
