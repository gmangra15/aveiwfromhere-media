Hormuz: October becomes the worst month yet for attacks on ships

Researchers counted a record 28 attacks on vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz in October, as an oil spill reached UAE beaches and Iran reportedly drew up plans to target undersea internet cables.

By Maps and Musings · 24 October 2026

The Strait of Hormuz, the narrow passage between Iran and Oman, has seen more attacks on shipping in October than in any month since the US-Israeli war with Iran began on 28 February.

A record month

Noam Raydan of the Washington Institute for Near East Policy recorded 28 confirmed and reported attacks in and around the strait in October, and 113 since the war began, Reuters reported. "October has seen the highest number of ship attacks on record," she said.

Greece-based consultancy Marisks said a dozen ships were attacked between 5 and 11 October, the highest weekly count of the conflict. Other trackers give different totals because of different methods and under-reporting: the International Maritime Organization lists 120 incidents across the strait and wider Middle East, and the UK's maritime trade agency (UKMTO) recorded 135 as of 16 October.

Supertankers in the line of fire

A Reuters review of IMO and MarineTraffic data found more than 30 very large crude carriers (VLCCs) damaged since the conflict began, 11 of them in October. That is about 3.5% of the world's VLCC fleet of more than 900 ships. Each can carry about two million barrels of crude. Daily freight rates have exceeded $1 million.

Raydan said Iran appears to be intensifying attacks to slow the recovery in shipments and keep pressure on energy markets ahead of the US midterm elections in November.

Oil reaches UAE beaches

On 23 October, the UAE's Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said it had detected a tanker oil spill affecting the beaches of Umm Al Quwain, an emirate north of Dubai, AFP reported. Containment and clean-up teams were deployed and the emirate's beaches were closed. The ministry did not name the tanker.

A threat beneath the sea

Separately, two senior Iranian security officials told Reuters that Tehran has detailed plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf if the US and Israel attack Iran again, and would try to block repairs to prolong outages. They gave few specifics.

Experts urge caution about the scale of the risk. Undersea cables carry about 99% of global internet traffic, according to the UN's International Telecommunication Union, but TeleGeography's Alan Mauldin told Reuters that less than 1% of international bandwidth passes through the Strait of Hormuz. The US State Department and Iran's foreign ministry did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment.

Why it matters

For energy importers such as Pakistan, the strait is a lifeline. Each new attack adds to shipping and insurance costs and can feed into fuel prices at home, which is why regional diplomats meeting in Istanbul this week called for the "swift and full resumption" of safe passage.
