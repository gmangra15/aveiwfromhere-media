Jordan strikes Syria after the Karak church attack: what we know

After a deadly attack on a church in Karak, Jordan launched air strikes in southern Syria. Syria says civilians were killed and denies any coordination, and the main border crossing is closed.

By Maps and Musings · 27 October 2026

Jordan has carried out air strikes in southern Syria, days after a deadly attack on a church in the southern Jordanian city of Karak.

What happened

Jordan says its jets targeted hideouts of armed groups operating across the border, following the attack on the church. Jordanian authorities have also arrested three people in connection with the attack.

On the Syrian side, Syrian civil defence says seven people were killed. Damascus denied any coordination with Amman over the strikes, and the Nassib–Jaber crossing, the main border post between the two countries and a key trade route, has been closed.

Why southern Syria matters to Jordan

Jordan shares a long desert border with Syria's Daraa and Suwayda provinces. For years, Amman has complained about smuggling networks moving weapons and the stimulant captagon across that frontier, and it has struck targets in southern Syria before.

The church attack has added a new and sensitive element: Jordan's Christian community is one of the oldest in the world, and Karak itself has a long Christian history.

What to watch

  • Whether the Nassib crossing reopens soon, since many Syrian and Jordanian traders and truckers depend on it.
  • Whether Damascus and Amman find a way to coordinate security on the border, or whether strikes continue.
  • Any claim of responsibility or further findings from the Jordanian investigation.
