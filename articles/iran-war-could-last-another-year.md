Eight months after the United States and Israel went to war with Iran, a senior American official has warned that the conflict could go on for another year.

## "Another year"

Mike Waltz, the US ambassador to the United Nations, told the Financial Times that the US would not achieve its key goals until Iran disposes of its highly enriched uranium and accepts a meaningful deal on domestic enrichment, according to CBS News. He said the US Navy could maintain its blockade of Iranian ports for the foreseeable future and that he hoped Iran would "come to the table" first.

President Donald Trump has said any new strikes would wait until after the US midterm elections on 3 November, while also claiming he could end the war quickly through military action.

## Tehran's reply

Iran's armed forces chief of staff, Ali Abdollahi, said US warships would never be allowed into the Persian Gulf "under any circumstances", CBS reported. A Revolutionary Guards spokesman dismissed US threats as largely for media consumption.

But Iran also signalled that diplomacy is not dead. According to CBS, Iran's ambassador to Pakistan said Tehran remains open to talks under the interim deal brokered by Pakistan in June.

## Oil and markets

Brent crude rose 3.5% on Friday to above **$117 a barrel**, its highest since March and up from under $97 at the start of October. US stocks fell. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains heavily disrupted, and October has seen a record number of attacks on vessels in and around the strait.

The US State Department is reducing staff at embassies in Jordan, Lebanon, Qatar and the UAE, after earlier ordering non-essential staff out of Saudi Arabia.

## Diplomacy in Istanbul

In Istanbul on Friday, foreign ministers from Pakistan, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia and Egypt met counterparts from Britain, France, Germany and Italy. France's Jean-Noël Barrot warned that escalation would "leave no winners". The regional group also backed the "Islamabad MoU", Pakistan's mediated understanding between Iran and the United States.

## What it means for Pakistan

Pakistan imports most of the oil it uses, so a long war means a long stretch of high fuel prices, pressure on the rupee and on inflation. At the same time, Islamabad's role as a channel between Washington and Tehran gives it an unusually central diplomatic position as both sides weigh their next moves.
