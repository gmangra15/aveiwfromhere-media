Locals call it the **land of giants**. Between **Skardu** in Baltistan and **Astore** in the west, the **Deosai Plains** stretch out at an average height of around 4,000 metres, making them one of the highest plateaus on Earth.

## A plateau under snow for half the year

For much of the year Deosai is buried in snow and the road across it is closed. In summer the snow melts into a wide, rolling grassland crossed by clear streams and carpeted with wildflowers. The best-known landmark is **Sheosar Lake**, near the western edge of the plains, whose still water reflects the surrounding peaks.

## Why it is a national park

Deosai was declared a **national park in 1993**, largely to protect the **Himalayan brown bear**, one of the rarest bears in the world. Pakistan's largest population of the species lives here. A 2022 survey by the Gilgit-Baltistan wildlife department counted **about 66 bears**, up from a few dozen when protection began. The park is also home to the Himalayan marmot, the red fox, the golden eagle and many migratory birds.

## New pressures

Success has brought its own problems. In summer, **Gujjar and Bakarwal herders** bring their cattle up to the plateau to graze, and the 2022 survey also counted **twelve campsites, sixty-six tents and nearly five thousand livestock** inside the park. As the Express Tribune reports, conservationists warn that overgrazing, disease passed from livestock, and rubbish left by tourists all threaten the bears' food and habitat. Dawn has also reported that nearby villagers worry about bears straying close to their homes and flocks.

## Visiting responsibly

Deosai is usually open from roughly **June to September**, reached by jeep from Skardu or from the Astore side. Visitors are asked to stay on tracks, keep their distance from wildlife, never feed animals and take all of their rubbish back with them.
