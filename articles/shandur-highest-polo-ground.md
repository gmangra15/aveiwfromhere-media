High in Pakistan's north, between Chitral and Gilgit-Baltistan, a wide grassy plateau opens out beside a still mountain lake. This is the **Shandur Pass**, at about **3,700 metres (12,000 feet)**, and every summer it becomes the setting for one of the world's most remarkable sporting events.

## The world's highest polo ground

The Shandur polo field is described by Arab News, Gulf News and the Associated Press as the **highest polo ground in the world**. Reaching it means a long drive up from the Laspur valley on the Chitral side, or from the Ghizer side in Gilgit-Baltistan, to a plateau framed by snow-capped peaks.

## A festival since 1936

The **Shandur Polo Festival** dates back to **1936**, according to Gulf News. It is held over three days in summer and draws thousands of local and international visitors. Alongside the matches there is folk music, traditional dancing, local food and handicrafts.

## Freestyle mountain polo

The polo played here is not the tightly regulated version seen in city clubs. **Freestyle mountain polo**, played in Pakistan's northern regions, is considered one of the oldest surviving forms of the game, with very few rules. Riders and horses compete at high altitude in thin air, which makes the matches as much a test of endurance as of skill.

## The great rivalry

The festival's centrepiece is the clash between **Chitral** and **Gilgit-Baltistan**, a rivalry Gulf News calls fierce. In 2026 the festival ran from 11 to 13 June, and in the final Gilgit-Baltistan beat Chitral **6-5** in a match lasting nearly an hour, their first title in more than a decade, according to Arab News and AP.

## Planning a visit

The festival is held in the short summer season, usually in June or July, when the pass is accessible. Nights at this altitude are cold even in summer, so bring warm clothing, and check road conditions and festival dates with local tourism authorities before travelling.

Polo is often called "the game of kings". At Shandur, as AP put it, it is above all a celebration of mountain communities and their heritage.
