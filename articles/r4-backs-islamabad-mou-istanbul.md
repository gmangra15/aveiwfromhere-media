Foreign ministers from Pakistan, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia and Egypt, a grouping now known as the **R4**, met in Istanbul on Friday, 23 October. Afterwards they held a first-ever session with Britain, France and Germany (the **E3**), joined by Italy.

## Support for the "Islamabad MoU"

According to the Express Tribune, the R4's joint statement "expressed support for the Islamabad MoU", the Pakistan-brokered understanding between Iran and the United States intended to keep the pause in hostilities from collapsing. The statement welcomed continued diplomacy to prevent escalation. The terms of the MoU have not been made public.

Pakistan was represented by Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, who briefed counterparts on Islamabad's mediation. Türkiye's Hakan Fidan hosted. Saudi Arabia's Prince Faisal bin Farhan and Egypt's Badr Abdelatty also attended.

## What the R4 said

The joint statement, as reported by the Express Tribune, covered four main areas:

- **Iran:** the conflict should be addressed only through dialogue and diplomacy, and attacks on civilians, civilian infrastructure and ships must be prevented.
- **Strait of Hormuz:** a call for the "swift and full resumption of safe, secure and unimpeded passage", citing risks to energy, food, fertiliser and supply chains.
- **Yemen:** strong condemnation of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and on shipping in the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandeb.
- **Palestine:** support for an independent Palestinian state on the 4 June 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as its capital, plus aid and reconstruction for Gaza.

## Europe joins the table

In the wider session, Fidan said none of the attending countries backed another military attack on Iran, according to The Nation. France's Jean-Noël Barrot said the eight countries had decided "to continue the work by meeting at regular intervals". Italy's Antonio Tajani said he did not believe the problem could be solved "from a purely military standpoint".

## Why it matters for Pakistan

Pakistan has been a key go-between for Washington and Tehran since the war began in February. Public backing from three major regional partners, followed by a seat at the table with Europe's leading powers, strengthens that role. The Foreign Office also said Pakistan has completed ratification of the Makkah defence agreement signed with Saudi Arabia and Türkiye in August, and that the pact is not directed against any other country.

## What to watch

Italy's Tajani warned before the meeting that hostilities in the Gulf could resume after the US elections in early November. Whether the Islamabad understanding holds through that period will be the real test of this new diplomacy.
